Patients

Fees & financial support

Fertility treatment can involve a mix of government-funded services, costs you pay yourself, medication and tax credits. This page explains each one in plain language. Your care team at True Care goes through the fees for your own plan before any treatment begins.

Book a ConsultationAsk reception: (289) 674-9676

Ontario-funded fertility treatment

Through the Ontario Fertility Program, the province funds certain fertility treatments — including one IVF cycle per eligible patient — at clinics that take part in the program. To be eligible you must live in Ontario and have a valid Ontario health card; age limits and other criteria apply. Medications are not included.

Funded treatment is provided only by participating clinics, which manage their own waitlists. Ask our team how funded and private treatment options compare for your situation.

Official information: Get fertility treatments (ontario.ca)

Costs you may pay yourself

Depending on your plan, these can include:

  • Fertility medications
  • Treatment cycles that aren’t publicly funded
  • Laboratory services such as ICSI, embryo freezing and storage, and genetic testing of embryos
  • Donor sperm or eggs, and agency or legal fees for donor and surrogacy treatment
  • Egg, sperm and embryo storage

Private treatment at True Care

Fees for privately paid treatment are explained before you begin, and you can ask for them in writing. For current fees for a specific service, ask reception at (289) 674-9676.

True Care accepts Blue Cross and Canadian Armed Forces coverage — ask reception what your plan includes.

Financial assistance and financing

Workplace benefits, health spending accounts and private insurance may pay for some medications or services. Ask our team whether payment plans or financing options are available for your treatment.

Medication

Fertility medications and insurance

Most fertility medications are prescribed for your cycle and paid at the pharmacy. Many private drug plans pay for some of them, but plans differ — check before your cycle starts.

  • Ask us for the names of the medications in your plan.
  • Call your insurer with the drug names and ask what is paid and whether pre-approval is needed.
  • Keep every pharmacy receipt for tax purposes.

Common types of fertility medication

Ovulation tablets
Letrozole or clomiphene, for ovulation induction
Stimulation injections
Hormones (gonadotropins) that help several eggs mature in IVF or egg freezing
Blocking injections
Medication that stops early ovulation during IVF stimulation
Trigger injection
Completes egg maturation before retrieval or IUI
Progesterone
Supports the lining of the uterus after embryo transfer

Tax credits

Fertility tax credits

Two programs can give some of your costs back. Check the official pages, and talk to a tax professional about your own return.

Ontario

Ontario Fertility Treatment Tax Credit

A refundable credit of 25% of eligible fertility treatment expenses, up to $20,000 a year — a maximum credit of $5,000 — for expenses paid on or after January 1, 2025. Eligible expenses are the same as for the medical expense tax credit.

Ontario Fertility Treatment Tax Credit (ontario.ca)

Federal

Medical expense tax credit

Fertility-related costs, such as IVF and related medical services prescribed by a medical practitioner, can count as eligible medical expenses on your federal and Ontario tax returns.

eligible medical expenses (canada.ca)

Information checked November 13, 2026. Government programs change — always confirm on the official pages.

Questions

Questions about costs

Will I know the cost before treatment starts?

Yes. Your care team goes through the fees for your plan before any treatment begins, and you can ask for them in writing.

Are fertility medications included in treatment fees?

Usually not — medications are prescribed separately and paid at the pharmacy. Check what your drug plan pays for before your cycle starts.

Can I claim fertility costs on my taxes?

Many fertility-related costs can count as medical expenses, and Ontario residents may also qualify for the Ontario Fertility Treatment Tax Credit for expenses paid from January 1, 2025. Keep every receipt and see the official guidance: Ontario Fertility Treatment Tax Credit (ontario.ca).
