Fertility · IVF

IVF and ICSI

In vitro fertilization (IVF) fertilizes eggs with sperm in the laboratory and transfers an embryo into the uterus. True Care offers conventional IVF and ICSI, embryo culture to blastocyst, fresh and frozen embryo transfer, embryo freezing, and IVF with anonymous donor eggs or sperm and gestational carriers, planned and monitored by Dr. Kattygnarath’s team.

When is IVF recommended?

IVF may be the first recommendation, or the next step after other treatment, for:

  • blocked or damaged fallopian tubes
  • significant sperm problems (with ICSI)
  • endometriosis
  • reduced ovarian reserve
  • unexplained infertility after unsuccessful IUI
  • preimplantation genetic testing of embryos
  • donor egg, donor sperm or gestational carrier treatment

What are the steps of an IVF cycle?

  1. Ovarian stimulation. Daily hormone injections, usually for around two weeks, help several eggs mature. Nurses teach you how to give injections.
  2. Monitoring. Ultrasound and bloodwork track follicle growth so doses can be adjusted — this also helps reduce the risk of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS).
  3. Trigger injection to complete egg maturation, timed precisely before retrieval.
  4. Egg retrieval. Eggs are collected with a fine needle guided by transvaginal ultrasound, under sedation.
  5. Fertilization. Eggs are combined with sperm (conventional IVF) or each mature egg is injected with a single sperm (ICSI).
  6. Embryo culture. Embryos develop in the laboratory, often to the blastocyst stage (day 5 or 6). Assisted hatching may be used where indicated.
  7. Embryo transfer. An embryo is placed in the uterus with a thin catheter — in the same cycle (fresh) or later (frozen).
  8. Pregnancy test by blood test about two weeks after transfer.

Embryos not transferred can be frozen by vitrification for future frozen embryo transfer (FET) cycles, which can use natural, modified natural or medicated protocols.

How many embryos are transferred?

Current professional guidance favours transferring a single embryo in most situations, because twin and higher-order pregnancies carry greater risks for both parent and babies. Your physician will discuss what is appropriate for your age and history.

Conventional IVF or ICSI?

In conventional IVF, each egg is placed with prepared sperm and fertilization happens on its own. In ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection), an embryologist injects one sperm directly into each mature egg. ICSI is used mainly for male-factor infertility or when fertilization failed in a previous cycle. Read more in ICSI versus conventional IVF.

Donor eggs, donor sperm and gestational carriers

True Care offers IVF with donor eggs and IVF with donor sperm — anonymous donors only — as well as gestational carrier cycles, with coordination and third-party reproduction counselling for everyone involved.

Questions

Common questions

How long does an IVF cycle take?

From starting stimulation medication to egg retrieval is usually about two weeks, with a blood pregnancy test roughly two weeks after transfer. A frozen embryo transfer is a separate, shorter cycle.

Is egg retrieval painful?

Retrieval is done under sedation, so most people are comfortable during it. Cramping and bloating for a day or two afterwards are common.

What are my chances of success?

Success depends mostly on age, egg and sperm factors, and the cause of infertility. Your physician will discuss realistic expectations for your situation after reviewing your results.

Can I use donor eggs or donor sperm?

Yes, with anonymous donors only. Gestational carrier cycles are also coordinated.
