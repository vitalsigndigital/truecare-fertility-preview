Fertility treatment options

Your options, in plain language

Fertility treatments have technical names. Here is what each one actually does — select any option for what it involves, who it may help and the main steps.

Helping you ovulateOvulation inductionMedication — usually tablets for a few days, sometimes injections — that helps one or two eggs mature and release, with ultrasound monitoring so the timing is right.Learn morePlacing prepared sperm into the uterusIntrauterine insemination (IUI)Prepared (washed) sperm is placed in the uterus with a thin, soft tube around the time you ovulate. It takes a few minutes and feels similar to a Pap test.Learn moreFertilizing eggs in the laboratoryIn vitro fertilization (IVF)Medication helps several eggs mature; the eggs are collected in a short procedure, fertilized with sperm in the laboratory, and an embryo is placed in the uterus.Learn moreInjecting a single sperm into an eggICSI, during IVFDuring IVF, an embryologist injects a single sperm directly into each mature egg. Everything else about the IVF cycle stays the same.Learn moreFreezing eggs for the futureEgg freezing & fertility preservationThe same medication and egg collection as IVF, then mature eggs are frozen very quickly (vitrification) and stored for later. Sperm and embryos can be frozen too.Learn moreUsing donor eggs, sperm or a surrogateThird-party reproductionTreatment that uses donated eggs, sperm or embryos, or a gestational carrier (surrogate) who carries the pregnancy. When to seek help

When should you see a fertility specialist?

Infertility affects about 1 in 6 people worldwide at some point in their lives (World Health Organization, 2023). Consider asking your doctor for a referral if you have been trying to conceive for:

  • 12 months if you are under 35
  • 6 months if you are 35 or older

Ask sooner if your periods are irregular or absent, you have known endometriosis or PCOS, you’ve had two or more pregnancy losses, your partner has a known sperm issue, you plan to use donor eggs or sperm, or you are about to start treatment that could affect fertility.

Questions

Fertility questions

What is the difference between IUI and IVF?

In IUI, prepared sperm is placed in the uterus and fertilization happens inside the body. In IVF, eggs are collected and fertilized in the laboratory, and an embryo is placed back in the uterus. IVF involves more steps and medication, and is used when IUI is unlikely to work or hasn’t worked.

Do you offer donor egg and donor sperm treatment?

Yes — donor sperm IUI, and IVF with donor eggs or donor sperm, all with anonymous donors only. Gestational carrier cycles are coordinated as well. See donors & surrogacy.

Do I need a referral for fertility care?

Yes — from your family doctor or nurse practitioner, faxed to (855) 694-9327. If you don’t have a referral yet, send a consultation request or call us and we’ll explain your options.

Can appointments be virtual?

Consultations and results reviews can often be done by video. Monitoring, bloodwork, ultrasound and procedures are done at the clinic.
