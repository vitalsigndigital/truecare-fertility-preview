Women’s health · Contraception

Contraception and IUDs

True Care offers the full range of contraception in Hamilton and Mississauga — hormonal and copper IUDs, contraceptive implants, pills, patches and rings, and emergency contraception — plus counselling and referral for permanent contraception. Your physician helps you choose a method that fits your health, plans and preferences.

Which contraception options are available?

Hormonal IUD
A small device releasing progestin; lasts several years depending on the device, and often makes periods lighter
Copper IUD
Hormone-free; lasts many years depending on the device
Contraceptive implant
A small rod placed under the skin of the upper arm; lasts up to three years
Pill, patch or ring
Combined or progestin-only hormonal methods you take, apply or insert regularly
Emergency contraception
Pills or a copper IUD after unprotected sex
Permanent contraception
Counselling and referral for tubal procedures

Emergency contraception

Emergency contraception works best the sooner it is used. Levonorgestrel pills are available at pharmacies without a prescription and are most effective within 72 hours; ulipristal acetate (by prescription) can be used up to 5 days after unprotected sex. A copper IUD inserted within 7 days is the most effective emergency option and then continues as ongoing contraception (SOGC).

What is IUD insertion like?

IUD insertion is a short in-office procedure. Most people feel cramping during insertion and for a short time afterwards. Your physician will talk through pain-relief options beforehand and explain how to check that the IUD is in place.

Planning a pregnancy later?

Fertility returns quickly after an IUD or implant is removed or the pill is stopped. If you’re thinking about pregnancy in the next year or two, mention it — a pre-conception or fertility assessment can be part of the same visit.

Questions

Common questions

Can I get an IUD if I’ve never been pregnant?

Yes. IUDs are suitable for most people, including those who have never been pregnant.

Will a hormonal IUD stop my periods?

It often makes periods lighter, and for some people they stop altogether. Irregular spotting is common in the first few months.

Do I need contraception during perimenopause?

Yes, until menopause is confirmed. See menopause care.

Ready to take the next step?
